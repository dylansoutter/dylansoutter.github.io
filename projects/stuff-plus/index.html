Stuff+

Turning physical pitch traits into a repeatable fastball-quality model for Georgia Tech Baseball.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • NumPy
  • XGBoost
  • scikit-learn

Overview

Stuff+ evaluates fastball quality using physical measurements such as release, spin, and movement. The pipeline turns pitch-level TrackMan data into repeatable pitcher rankings for Georgia Tech Baseball coaches.

My Role

Within Georgia Tech’s VIP Baseball Analytics team, I led a four-person Stuff+ subgroup and contributed most of the modular Python pipeline: data processing, feature engineering, model workflows, and repeatable leaderboard generation.

This is collaborative research. The broader VIP team contributes baseball context, shared infrastructure, and review.

Data

The original 2020–2022 source contained approximately 1,048,575 rows, with approximately 582,970 fastball-family pitches after the initial filter.

Those are source-stage counts, not the final training sample. The current baseline further restricts the population to Division I and applies deduplication and cleaning.

Pipeline

  1. 01TrackMan data

    Select physical pitch measurements.

  2. 02Clean & normalize

    Deduplicate, filter pitch families, mirror handedness.

  3. 03Engineer features

    Represent release, spin, and movement.

  4. 04Train & evaluate

    Train on 2020–21; evaluate on 2022.

  5. 05Aggregate & rank

    Turn pitcher-team averages into a reference scale.

Modeling

The current baseline uses XGBoost to estimate the probability of a called or swinging strike (CSW). Horizontal measurements are mirrored to a common handedness, and spin axis is represented with sine and cosine.

Raw plate location, count, sequencing, and batter identity are excluded from model inputs. Adjusted approach-angle features are compared against a no-angle model before selection.

Results

The pipeline generates pitcher-team leaderboards for review by Georgia Tech Baseball coaches. It makes the path from raw input to final ranking repeatable and easier to inspect.

On the current reference scale, 100 is the mean predicted CSW for qualifying 2022 D1 pitcher-team groups; ten points represents one standard deviation. It is a reference-based grade, not a guarantee of performance.
